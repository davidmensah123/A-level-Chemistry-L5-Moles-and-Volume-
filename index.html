01 Solution Concentration & Standard Solutions

**Concentration** describes how much solute is dissolved per unit volume of a solution. In chemistry, the standard SI volume unit is the **cubic decimetre** (\(\text{dm}^3\)), where:

Volume Conversion \(1\text{ dm}^3 = 1\text{ L} = 1000\text{ cm}^3\)
Concentration (\(c\)) \[c = \frac{n}{V} \quad (\text{mol dm}^{-3})\]
Amount in Moles (\(n\)) \[n = c \times V \quad (\text{with } V \text{ in dm}^3)\]

What is a Standard Solution?

A **standard solution** is a solution of accurately known concentration. It is prepared by dissolving an exact, known mass of primary standard solute in deionized water and carefully making the solution up to an exact calibrated mark in a **volumetric flask**.

Two-step calculation pipeline:
1. Compute moles required: \(n = c \times V(\text{dm}^3)\)
2. Compute mass required: \(m = n \times M_r\)

02 Molar Gas Volume at RTP

Avogadro’s Law dictates that **equal volumes of all gases under the exact same temperature and pressure contain an equal number of moles (or molecules)**.

RTP Definition

Room Temperature & Pressure

  • **Temperature:** \(20^\circ\text{C}\) (\(293\text{ K}\))
  • **Pressure:** \(1\text{ atm}\) (\(\approx 101.3\text{ kPa}\) or \(100\text{ kPa}\))
  • **Dry Gas:** Assuming no water vapour partial pressure.
Molar Volume (\(V_m\))

\(24.0\text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}\)

At RTP, one mole of any gas occupies exactly \(24.0\text{ dm}^3\) (\(24,000\text{ cm}^3\)).

\[n = \frac{V(\text{dm}^3)}{24.0} \quad \iff \quad V = n \times 24.0\text{ dm}^3\]

03 The Ideal Gas Equation (\(pV = nRT\))

When conditions deviate from standard room temperature and pressure, the molar volume \(24.0\text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}\) cannot be applied. Instead, we use the **Ideal Gas Equation**:

\[pV = nRT\]

Where \(R = 8.31\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}\) is the universal ideal gas constant.

Strict SI Unit Conversions
Quantity Symbol Mandatory SI Unit
Pressure\(p\)Pascals (\(\text{Pa}\)) • \(1\text{ kPa} = 1000\text{ Pa}\)
Volume\(V\)Cubic metres (\(\text{m}^3\)) • \(1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ dm}^3 = 10^6\text{ cm}^3\)
Moles\(n\)\(\text{mol}\)
Gas Constant\(R\)\(8.31\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}\)
Temperature\(T\)Kelvin (\(\text{K}\)) • \(T(\text{K}) = \theta(^\circ\text{C}) + 273\)
Kinetic Theory Assumptions

Ideal Gas Postulates

  • Molecules move in continuous, rapid, random straight-line motion.
  • Collisions between particles and with container walls are **perfectly elastic**.
  • The volume of gas molecules is **negligible** compared to the volume of the container.
  • There are **no intermolecular forces** between particles.
